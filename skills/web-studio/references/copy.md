# Copy system

Write the copy before the layout. Real words decide the layout: line lengths, emphasis, how much space a section needs. Output: `./_studio/content.md`, with every page and section in order and its final copy. Use placeholders only for facts the client must supply, marked `[[client: ...]]`.

## Voice
- Derive it from the narrative's personality ("confident, not loud"). Write 3 rules, for example: short sentences; numbers over adjectives; no exclamation marks.
- One term per thing: if it's a "bag", it's never also a "pack" or "pouch".

## Headlines
Approaches that work (choose per section, don't repeat one approach everywhere):
- **The specific claim:** a concrete, checkable statement. ("At your door 48 hours after roasting.")
- **The reframe:** turn the category's assumption around. ("Bitter isn't strong. It's stale.")
- **The contrast:** two ideas in tension. ("Loud coffee for quiet mornings.")
- **The number:** a real figure with meaning. ("12 kilos at a time. Never more.")
- **The command:** an action with a benefit. ("Taste the roast date.")

Length: hero headline 2–8 words; section headlines under 10. Read it out loud; if you stumble, rewrite.

Never use (instant template tells): unlock, elevate, seamless, revolutionise, empower, next-level, game-changing, one-stop, world-class, cutting-edge, "Welcome to …", "We are passionate about …", "Your journey starts here", stacked adjectives.

## Subheads (hero and sections)
In 25 words or fewer, say what it is, who it's for and why it's different. The headline makes them feel; the subhead makes them understand.

## Section patterns
- **Value points:** a benefit headline plus one line of proof each. Max 3–4; if there are more, it's a list, not a section.
- **How it works:** 3 steps, each starting with a verb, each under 12 words.
- **Product card:** name; the one-line reason to choose it; 2–3 specifics (notes, size, origin); price; action.
- **Proof:** numbers with context ("4,812 bags shipped since March"). Testimonials quote a specific result, not "great service!", with name and role. Use press logos only if real.
- **FAQ:** the real objections (price, shipping, returns, how to choose), answered directly in 1–3 sentences.
- **CTA block:** one action, named exactly ("Start a subscription"), plus a reassurance line ("Skip or cancel any time").

## Microcopy
- **Buttons:** verb + object ("Add to bag", "Book a viewing"). Never "Submit" or "Click here".
- **Errors:** say what happened and how to fix it. **Empty states:** invite the next action.
- **Labels:** sentence case, no ALL-CAPS eyebrows above every heading.

## The specificity ladder
Climb every line as high as the facts allow:
"Great coffee" → "Specialty coffee" → "Single-origin specialty coffee" → "Washed Ethiopian, roasted Tuesday" → "Washed Guji, 2,100 m, roasted Tuesday, tastes like peach and black tea".

## Editing pass (always)
1. Cut 30%.
2. Replace every adjective you can with a fact.
3. Remove repeated ideas between sections.
4. Read the page top to bottom as the visitor: does each section answer the question the previous one raised?
5. Check terms, numbers and CTAs are consistent everywhere.

## Before / after (invented)
- Before: "Welcome to our passionate world of premium artisan coffee." → After: "Coffee tastes like its roast date. Ours ships two days after it."
- Before: "Our innovative solutions empower your team." → After: "Close the month in 3 days, not 10."
- Before: "Luxury living redefined." → After: "Every apartment faces the water. All 214 of them."
